\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Superoptimization.} Massalin~\cite{massalin} exhausted a 68020 subset and verified by inspection; Bansal and Aiken~\cite{bansalaiken} enumerated three-instruction x86 peepholes with zChaff equivalence and hand-argued pruning they note is unsound for runtime cost; Denali asked a solver \enquote{no program computes $P$ in $\le k$ cycles} and discarded the refutation; STOKE makes no minimality claim; Souper's CEGIS is minimality-shaped and uncertified and names proof-producing solvers as future work; Minotaur~\cite{minotaur} formalizes 165 x86 SIMD intrinsics in Alive2, claims profitability under LLVM's cost model, not minimality, and concedes \enquote{bugs might remain \ldots in Alive2, or in Z3}; SuperStack~\cite{superstack} gives a five-way taxonomy of how superoptimizers become incomplete and carries an \code{isOptimal} flag that is the solver's word; HieraSynth~\cite{hierasynth} proves cost-model optimality for 7--8-instruction programs over 700-instruction sets with five trust assumptions and no artifact. None emits a checkable minimality certificate. We concede scale to HieraSynth entirely and compete on an axis it has no point on.

\paragraph{Certified correctness.} Alive2~\cite{alive2}, CompCert~\cite{compcert}, Six et al.'s certified scheduling~\cite{six2020}, and CryptOpt~\cite{cryptopt} certify that generated code is \emph{right}; CryptOpt in particular already argues that \enquote{the random-search procedures need not be trusted} and then checks only equivalence. We certify the other half.

\paragraph{Certified minimality elsewhere.} Codish et al. proved $S(9)=25$ by SAT and Cruz-Filipe et al. gave a formally verified proof of that lower bound~\cite{sortingnetworks}; the Kochen--Specker lower bound shipped a 40\,TiB certificate; VeriPB and CakePB certify pseudo-Boolean optimality~\cite{veripb}, and DRCP does the same for constraint programming with a Rocq-verified checker. This machinery has never been pointed at an instruction set. Franchetti and Püschel~\cite{franchetti2008} is the antecedent we extend: stride permutations, SSE2 and Cell, a human-readable lower bound. An Isabelle/AFP entry (2026) certifies minimum \emph{tree}-cost e-graph extraction; the NP-hard DAG-cost case has no certificate.

\paragraph{Shuffle lowering.} LLVM's x86 shuffle lowering has no paper: some thirty-eight hand-written strategies tried in fixed order, a hard combine depth of eight, and an in-source \enquote{FIXME: We will currently miss some cases}; 144 tracked missed-optimization issues mention shuffles. LLVM does compute exhaustive-within-an-operator-set tables for four-lane NEON shuffles (\code{PerfectShuffle.cpp}), with a non-additive cost model and no certificate. Swizzle Inventor computes real UNSAT results for GPU swizzles and discards them.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What a certificate certifies.} Optimality relative to a named language and a named cost model, nothing more. Unison reports 81\% of functions proven optimal under a constant-latency model and 17.7\% slower on silicon; both are true. We do not claim certified-optimal code is faster. We claim that the statement \enquote{no cheaper sequence exists in $S$ under $M$} is now a checkable object rather than a sentence, and that $S$ and $M$ should ship with it.

\paragraph{The cost of the certificate.} Search produced each proof in under two seconds; checking each takes under a second. This is the regime the stack is designed for. It does not scale unboundedly: the same stack's largest certificate to date (a Rado number) took 8,762\,s to produce and 14,500\,s to check, and backward checking measured 6.6--10$\times$ proof size in RAM. Certificates of the size in \cref{tab:theorems} are deliberate.

\paragraph{The base rate.} How often is \enquote{proved optimal} wrong when nobody checks? In the 2016 MiniZinc Challenge, 5 of 22 solvers falsely claimed optimality; a 2022 audit found at least 45 of 3,500 claimed solutions incorrect across solvers; the 2025 challenge results were amended in January 2026 for \enquote{undetected incorrect optimality claims}; MIPLIB removed 328 instances for cross-solver inconsistency and notes that \enquote{the definition of the optimal objective value \ldots is ambiguous}. Compilers share a handful of backends and cost models, so correlated error is the expected case, not the tail.

\paragraph{Next.} A certified optimality census over production shuffle patterns---Highway, simdjson, LLVM's own test suite---reporting LLVM's sequence, the certified optimum, and the certificate. The rows where LLVM is already optimal are the unique contribution: any superoptimizer can show LLVM is beatable; none can show it is exactly optimal in a way a third party can check. A three-profile cost-model sweep, and the RISC-V table certified, follow.
