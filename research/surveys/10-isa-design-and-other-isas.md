# Certified minimal instruction sequences: ISA design, other ISAs, and 25 open problems

*Produced 2026-08-27 by an Opus 5 research agent (SerpApi Google + Scholar, GitHub API, `curl | pdftotext`; WebSearch exhausted early). Two strands (quantum/reversible; exotic ISAs) delegated to parallel agents and folded in. Reproduced verbatim including Part B, whose computations were re-implemented and re-run in this repository (`scripts/`, `artifacts/riscv/`).*

---

## 0. Headline findings

1. **Real ISA-design documents are full of instruction-count claims, including bare lower-bound assertions, and not one is certified.** The RISC-V Bitmanip draft: *"Using only base-ISA instructions, at least 7 instructions are needed to pack a 5:6:5 RGB value"* — an unproven lower bound, in a specification, used to justify instructions.
2. **The authors of RISC-V's own crypto extensions say the field has not made the transition to machine-checked rigor.** Cheng, Großschädl, Marshall, Page & Saarinen, *SoK: Instruction Set Extensions for Cryptographers*, TCHES / ePrint 2024/1323: *"it seems unlikely that a definitive approach, or codified advice that 'solves' the ISE design problem does or even can exist"* and *"our survey identified few instances of (academic) research that included a formal, machine-readable ISE specification."*
3. **An ISA-design decision resting on an uncertified exhaustive search, in the official RISC-V crypto repository.** `doc/supp/bitlogic.adoc` tabulates, for all 256 three-input Boolean functions, the claimed minimum RISC-V instruction count with and without Zbkb's `andn`/`orn`/`xnor`, and uses it to answer *"Would NOR and NAND instructions help? Not really."* Only stated evidence: *"our exhaustive search"* — no code, no artifact.
4. **The agent independently reproduced these claims by exhaustive search — which is exactly the point.** Two uncertified exhaustive searches agreeing is still not a proof.
5. **The artifact behind the Bitmanip permutation tables is gone.** Reference [26], *"Brute-force enumeration of bit permutations reachable with rot/grev/[un]shfl"*, points at `svn.clairexen.net/handicraft/2019/permexplore`, now **404** (as does [25], `lut4perm`). The instructions it justified (`grev`, `shfl`) were dropped when the task group dissolved.
6. **Across quantum synthesis, reversible synthesis, EVM/Wasm superoptimization, FPGA exact synthesis, GPU work, and the classic superoptimizer line — ~30 flagged claims — the number carrying a machine-checkable certificate for the nonexistence-of-shorter half is zero.** Including Toffoli T-count = 7.
7. **The field has split the guarantee in half, and nobody holds both:**

| | Equivalence certified | Minimality certified |
|---|---|---|
| **CryptOpt** (PLDI 2023) — Coq-verified checker | yes | no claim |
| **BCF** (SOSP 2025) — in-kernel checker | yes (safety) | no claim |
| **FORVES** (CAV 2023) — Coq, EVM blocks | yes | no claim |
| Denali, Bansal–Aiken, Brahma, Optgen, SuperStack, GASOL, Swizzle Inventor, Knuth's table, `percy`, Gosset et al., Bravyi et al., … | solver's word | solver's word |
| **This paper** | — | **the empty cell** |

## Part A. How ISA extensions are actually justified

**A.1 RISC-V Bitmanip: quantitative in draft, silent once ratified.** The v0.93 draft: *"at least 7 instructions are needed"* (§4.1.3, **lower bound, unproven**); *"arbitrary bit-permutations… 18 instruction (32 bit) or 22 instructions (64 bits)"* (§2.9.1, Beneš upper bound); *"ror, grev, and [un]shfl can perform any of those permutations in at most 3 instructions"* (§4.2.2, Table 4.1, brute force [26]); *"40320 ways of arranging the eight bytes… in at most 9 instructions"* (§2.2); `xperm.n` arbitrary 64-bit permutation in 15 instructions (§2.5, [25]); Table 2.2 reachability counts `1, 62, 864, 4640, 23312, …` and `1, 85, 3030, 78659, 2002167, 50106844, 1234579963`. Not one claimed minimal; the spec disclaims its own examples: *"They are not optimal implementations."* **The ratified text discards all of it**: `overview.adoc` justifies the family as *"some combination of code size reduction, performance improvement, and energy reduction"*; `zba.adoc`'s rationale is hardware critical path. Both artifact URLs return 404 (verified `curl -I`, 2026-08-27). riscv-bitmanip#151: the task group *"was dissolved once the original BitManip Extensions … were ratified."* One count survives: Zbb `strlen.adoc`, *"Our critical loop is 4 instructions."*

**A.2 RISC-V Zicond.** The ratified spec's **Length column**: conditional add/sub/or/xor 2 insns; conditional and 3 (1 temporary); **conditional select 3 insns, 1 temporary.** Never claims minimality; exactly the contested point in the Zicond-vs-`cmov` argument (HN thread: *"branchless sequences GCC produced for Zicond were never better and sometimes worse than the equivalent branching sequence"*).

**A.3 RISC-V crypto.** Rationale appendix is provenance prose, no counts; vector-crypto specs contain **no justification at all.** The SoK quote above.

**A.4 Intel APX / AVX10, Arm SVE2 / Helium.** Aggregate benchmark deltas (APX: "10% fewer loads, >20% fewer stores"), narrative rationale, hardware-cost reasoning. **Never a certified per-idiom minimum.** Nothing in the methodology would detect an instruction that is redundant because a shorter existing sequence was overlooked.

## Part B. The agent's own measurements

*(Re-implemented in this repository: `scripts/bitlogic_bfs.py`, `scripts/byte_perm_bfs.py`. Results: `artifacts/riscv/*.json`; objects `M.riscv.bitlogic256`, `M.riscv.byteperm24`, `M.riscv.table22`.)*

**B.1 The 256-function bitlogic table is correct and optimal.** Subject: `bitlogic.adoc` (Saarinen; two commits, both 2020-06-28). Step 1, semantic check: all 256 rows' C expressions evaluate to the stated truth table under x=0xAA, y=0xCC, z=0xF0 — 0 errors. Step 2, cost-model inference: the `Len` column is straight-line-program length **with sharing** (a Boolean chain, = Knuth's C(f)) — not stated in the document and necessary to re-derive it. Step 3, exhaustive BFS over frozensets of held truth tables: extended set all 256 at ≤ 4 (histogram 0:5, 1:27, 2:112, 3:88, 4:24); base set ≤ 5 (0:5, 1:12, 2:42, 3:112, 4:73, 5:12). **0 entries beatable in either column; the document is right on all 512.** Step 4: for SHA-2 `Ch` (0xD8) and `Maj` (0xE8) the minima are **3 and 4 in both sets** — `andn`/`orn`/`xnor` buy zero instructions; the gain is depth only. **Not verified:** the `Dep` column.

**B.2 Bitmanip byte-permutation claim, plus per-permutation minima.** Implemented `ror`, `grev`, `shfl`, `unshfl` per the spec's pseudocode; 92 concrete instructions as bit-position permutations; BFS. A methodological correction: the first attempt tested byte *values* rather than bit *positions* and wrongly accepted `GREV(1)`. Result: all 24 reached, **max 3; per-permutation minima 1 op: 6, 2 ops: 9, 3 ops: 8** (spec omits these).

**B.3 Exact reproduction of the lost Table 2.2.** ROT+GREV: `1 62 864 4640 23312 92192 294992 703744 1012856 1046224 1048576` — **11/11 match**; ROT+GREV+SHFL to depth 4: `1 85 3030 78659 2002167` — **5/5 match.** Recovers numbers whose source is 404. *"A DRAT-style artifact would have made this a five-second check instead of a research task."*

**B.4 The bit-address algebra that makes the SAT encoding small.** `ROR(k)`: address → `(addr − k) mod 32`. `GREV(k)`: address → `addr XOR k`. `SHFL(k)`: one of 16 distinct permutations of the 5 address bits. **The whole bit-permutation-synthesis problem is a 5-bit (RV32) / 6-bit (RV64) word-level problem** — a few thousand SAT variables where BFS on the 64-bit case is ~10¹³ states.

## Part C. Custom-instruction / ASIP synthesis
"Exact" means complete enumeration of candidate subgraphs, never minimality of resulting code (Atasu/Pozzi/Ienne; Xiao & Casseau 2012). Current SOTA: **æSIP** (ISCA 2026) — e-graphs + ILP extraction, 17% area reduction, no sequence-minimality claim; **CIExplorer** (ICS 2025) — frankly heuristic. **Gap:** nobody asks *"what is the provable minimum number of base instructions for the idiom this candidate replaces?"* — the number that decides whether an instruction earns its opcode.

## Part D. Micro-op fusion
Canonical justification is an unrefereed tech report (Celio et al., arXiv:1607.02318, 2016): 5.4% effective instruction-count reduction, idioms chosen by inspecting SPEC. Follow-on is measurement (Singh et al., MICRO 2022; RISC-V crypto `fusion.adoc`, WIP/DRAFT July 2020 — hand-derived cycle savings, never bounds). **"Minimum uops" is well-posed for a fixed published fusion table and unstudied there** (OP-11).

## Part E. Quantum circuits as an ISA
**Zero papers or tools emit a machine-checkable lower-bound certificate.** Strongest instance: Gouzien & Sangouard, *Provably optimal exact gate synthesis*, arXiv:2503.15452 `[PREPRINT]` — runs **Kissat**, which emits DRAT natively, cites the SAT Competition proof-checker volume, and never turns the flag on. **The field has already paid:** ASP-DAC 2023's Clifford SAT paper carries a correction — *"scalability is corrected from 27 to 6 qubits"* — three years post-publication. Proven analytically: 2-qubit generic needs 3 CNOTs; Toffoli CNOT count 6; T-count over {CNOT,T} is Reed–Muller decoding (Amy–Mosca 2019); Clifford synthesis is in NP (Peham et al. 2023) — the right class for DRAT. Proven by exhaustive search, no certificate: Toffoli T-count 7 (Gosset et al. 2014); all 6-qubit optimal Cliffords via a 2.1 TB table (Bravyi et al. 2022); all 16! 4-bit reversible permutations over NCT, max 15 (Golubitsky & Maslov 2012). **Toffoli T-count with one ancilla and feedback: best 4, proven 3 — a gap of one, open 13 years.** Clifford tables stop at n=6 (n=7 open, |C₇| ≈ 10³²). Reversible exhaustive frontier permanently at 4 bits (32! ≈ 2.6·10³⁵).

## Part F. Other exotic ISAs
**SuperStack** (PLDI 2024): carries an explicit `isOptimal` boolean — the solver's word; **most retrofittable target** now that MaxSAT optimality proof logging exists. **ebso / GASOL / syrup**: Z3/Max-SMT "gas-optimal," no certificates. **A revealing split:** the EVM community verifies the wrong half — FORVES (CAV 2023) and follow-ups check block *equivalence* in Coq while optimality rests on the solver. **eBPF Merlin** (ASPLOS 2024): no minimality claim. **Minotaur**: *"bugs might remain … in Alive2, or in Z3."* **SIMD shuffles**: the state of the art is a hand-rolled BFS on a personal page (Meunier, LIP6). **Boolean chains / Knuth §7.1.2**: Goucher's `Optimal5` (9.2 MB `knuthies.dat` — a lookup table, not a proof); depth 9 ≈ 11 TB; Goucher states the ISA connection outright — the 5 normal gates *"correspond to AVX instructions vpxor, vpand, vpor, vpandn"*. **Knuth's C(f) is a minimum AVX instruction count**, and AVX-512's `vpternlog` collapses the RISC-V bitlogic table to 1. **SAT-based exact synthesis** (Haaswijk/Soeken/Mishchenko/De Micheli; `percy`/`mockturtle`): no proofs emitted anywhere in this line.

## Part G. GPU and the classic superoptimizer line
**SASS/PTX superoptimization does not exist**; root cause: no vendor-published SASS semantics, so you cannot declare a subset — the precondition for a certified claim. PTXASW (CC 2023): Z3 picks shuffle deltas, no certificate. **Swizzle Inventor** (ASPLOS 2019) already computes real UNSAT results and discards them (Table 1: *"time to prove that no solution exists in that space"*). Mirage (OSDI 2025): probabilistic correctness. Warp-shuffle/`LOP3`/tensor-core minimality: nothing published. Classic superoptimizers, precise wording: Massalin (test vectors); Denali (*"near-optimal"*, axiom-relative); Denali-2 (design in future tense); Bansal–Aiken (zChaff equivalence only; hand-argued pruning the authors flag unsound for runtime cost); Brahma (component multiset); STOKE (*"to the best of our knowledge, it is truly optimal"*); Optgen (cost limit 2); Blindell (Chuffed is LCG — retrofit target); Souper (names proof-producing solvers as future work, for equivalence only); CryptOpt (Coq equivalence, no minimality); Cowan et al. CGO 2020; Aho & Johnson JACM 1976 (the one honest entry — a theorem). **Certificate technology exists outside compilers and has never been pointed at this**: VeriPB/CakePB, certified MaxSAT, Pareto optimality (TACAS 2025), PB proof logging for planning (ICAPS 2025). Kochen–Specker at 40.3 TiB is *"the first computer-verifiable proof certificate of a lower bound."* **Frame the contribution as technology transfer, not invention.** Near-precedent to cite: Busnello, *The Unit Gap Is Base-Dependent* (self-hosted, unrefereed) — certifies `opt(⊕₃)=6` in the AIG model with drat-trim.

## Part H. Twenty-five open problems (condensed)

**CPU / ISA.** OP-1 certify the 256-function bitlogic table, both sets (**days**; <1 GB total; ⭐ flagship). OP-2 Zicond conditional select: is 3 minimal? (weeks). OP-3 prove/refute "at least 7" for RGB 5:6:5 (weeks; ⭐ highest rhetorical value). OP-4 24 byte permutations over {ror,grev,shfl,unshfl} certified (**days**; ⭐ cleanest transplant). OP-5 "at most 9" for 40320 nibble permutations — where BFS dies and SAT lives (weeks–months). OP-6 arbitrary data-dependent permutation: is 18/22 optimal? (Σ₂; new theory / months restricted). OP-7 does Zbkb reduce any crypto-relevant 3-input function? (days, given OP-1). OP-8 minimum AVX2/AVX-512/NEON/SVE2 counts for named shuffles, cross-ISA (weeks per ISA; ⭐ breadth). OP-9 ChaCha20 quarter-round / Keccak steps on RV32I (months). OP-10 certify Knuth's C(f) as an AVX instruction-count result (weeks–months). OP-11 certified minimum fused-domain µops for a fixed fusion table (months; most novel formulation). OP-12 re-derive Table 2.2 as a replacement artifact with a closure-transcript certificate (weeks; pilot for the format).

**Quantum / reversible.** OP-13 Toffoli T-count with ancilla: 3 or 4? (months). OP-14 {CNOT,T} T-count for 6/7-variable functions via certified RM decoding (weeks; ⭐ best fully verified quantum result). OP-15 NCT gate count for Ascon S-box / Keccak χ₅ / hwb5 (weeks; ⭐ top quantum flagship). OP-16 certified 7-qubit Clifford re-runs — Q-Synth + CaDiCaL `--binary` flag (weeks; ⭐ lowest-hanging fruit in the report). OP-17 small Cliffords under LNN/heavy-hex (weeks). OP-18 exact T-count constants for 3/4-bit adders (weeks–months). OP-19 re-establish "max 4-bit NCT = 15" with an artifact (months; ⭐ cleanest illustration of the thesis). OP-20 CNOT-count diameter of GL(7,2) (weeks per-instance; months + new format for the diameter).

**GPU / classic.** OP-21 minimum `LOP3` count for all 222 4-input NPN classes (weeks; highest-value GPU result). OP-22 minimum `shfl.sync` steps for a fixed 32-lane permutation — counting bound proves blending essential (weeks–months). OP-23 minimum `prmt.b32` count for a 4×4 byte transpose (weeks). OP-24 certificate-carrying Optgen at cost 3 (weeks). OP-25 DRAT-certified replacement for Bansal–Aiken's length-2 table (months; certificate-aggregation theory).

**Two methodological warnings.** (a) **Plain DRAT is the wrong format for much of this**: these instances are saturated with symmetry, and VeriPB's dominance rules have never been applied to instruction/circuit/quantum synthesis. (b) **Instance-wide and group-wide claims need different certificates**: "every 4-bit permutation is reachable in ≤15 gates" quantifies over 10¹³ objects; the right artifact is a streaming closure transcript with a checkable frontier invariant plus a cardinality check. Arc: OP-12 → OP-19 → OP-20 / OP-25.
