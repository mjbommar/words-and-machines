# Chapter 3 timeline

Dates organize research; they do not by themselves authorize priority claims.

| Date | Event | Relevance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1945 | The EDVAC draft organizes stored orders by operation and movement roles | An instruction needs operational meaning and a way to identify its inputs and results | EDVAC report, sections 14--15 |
| 1953 | Wilkes and Stringer publish a microprogrammed control design | One architectural instruction can be realized by a sequence of lower-level control actions | Wilkes and Stringer paper |
| 1964 | IBM publishes the System/360 architecture and principles manuals | Regular RR, RX, RS, SI, and SS formats support a compatible family while distinguishing operand roles | IBM paper and manual |
| 1981 | Patterson and Sequin present RISC I | Instruction regularity and register-oriented operand choices become explicit VLSI design arguments | RISC I paper |
| 2000s--2026 | GCC machine descriptions and LLVM TableGen maintain target instruction knowledge | Current compilers require structured operand, effect, constraint, encoding, and selection information | Official GCC and LLVM documentation |
| 2026 | Current RISC-V and Intel manuals specify contrasting addition forms | Three explicit register operands and two-address-plus-flags forms expose different state contracts | Pinned official manuals |
