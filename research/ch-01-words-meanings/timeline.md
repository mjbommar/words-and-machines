# Chapter 1 timeline

Dates organize research; they do not by themselves authorize manuscript
claims.

| Date | Event | Relevance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1703 | Leibniz publishes his explanation of binary arithmetic | Positional rules with the characters 0 and 1 | Leibniz paper |
| 1801 | Gauss publishes *Disquisitiones Arithmeticae* | Congruence supplies the language for changing representatives without changing a residue | Gauss, sections 1--3 |
| 1854 | Boole publishes *Laws of Thought* | Logical relations receive an algebraic treatment | Boole book |
| 1938 | Shannon publishes his symbolic analysis of relay and switching circuits | Symbolic logic becomes a method for analyzing, synthesizing, and minimizing physical switching networks | Shannon paper |
| 1940s--1960s | Computer families use several signed representations | Signed representation was an engineering choice, not a fact inherent in binary | UNIVAC 1108 manual supplies a primary ones'-complement example |
| 1964 | System/360 specifies two's-complement binary integer arithmetic | A durable architecture fixes representation as a software contract | System/360 primary documents |
| 2019--2026 | Ratified RISC-V revisions specify RV64 word-result and shift rules | Width remains visible in a modern open ISA | Pinned RISC-V manual |
| 2026 | Current Intel manuals retain operand-size, subregister, shift, and rotate rules | Historical compatibility keeps width rules industrially active | Intel SDM version 092 |
