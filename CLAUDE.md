# AI Instructions — Instruction Sets, Programs, and Proofs

Read this, then `book/docs/SPIRIT.md`, `book/docs/guides/VOICE.md`,
`book/docs/guides/CRAFT.md`, and `book/docs/guides/PLAIN-ENGLISH.md` before
writing a word of prose. The book-specific writing rules live in
`book/CLAUDE.md`; this file adds the rules that make this repository different
from an ordinary book.

## This repository

- **Premise:** every machine claim is an object; every machine-backed claim has
  an artifact and a checker that can fail.
- **Method:** Spivak's, not his table of contents. Build from a tiny defined
  base, prove load-bearing claims, and let exercises carry the subject. The
  final boundary chapter names the state and semantics that the scalar model
  deliberately omits.
- **Reader:** compiler, crypto-engineering, and formal-methods people who would read HieraSynth or SLOTHY and wonder what "optimal" meant there.
- **Foundation:** construct an architecture-neutral machine before named ISA
  case studies. Treat RISC and CISC as clusters of explicit semantic features.
  RISC-V and x86-64 are sustained contrasts. Optional extensions belong only
  where the book explicitly extends the scalar model.
- **Status:** eighteen evidence routes are replayable: thirteen A0 routes plus
  source-pin and executable decoder/step routes for the RV64I and x86-64
  teaching slices, plus the cross-machine absolute-value route. The real-ISA routes execute every selected form and all
  printed programs, check declared traps and canonical projections, and reject
  source-digest and branch-base mutations. The reusable A0 routes include the
  word-operation audit, canonical complete-state codec, and fixed-width
  addition and memory-frame certificates for every supported A0 width. General
  theorems for the remaining A0 operations remain open. One typed Rust
  cross-machine route now replays the printed absolute-value programs at named
  synchronization points for ten distinct 64-bit inputs; it is a finite
  computation, not a universal simulation theorem. The finite A0 equivalence
  route checks the Chapter 11 clear-register pair over its declared width-eight
  state families and decodes and replays two complete-state counterexamples.
  It is not a general equivalence solver or certificate. Axeyum's Python layer now
  exposes the complete concrete A0 word, memory, state-codec, instruction,
  step, trap, and bounded-trace surface. The
  Chapter 6 addition listing runs unchanged against that interface. The
  source-pinned RV64I and x86-64 teaching slices now expose typed instructions,
  canonical encode/decode, complete state, traps, projection, and single-step
  execution through Python. Real-ISA bounded traces and cross-machine Python
  relation projection and the Chapter 15 XOR relation remain open. The full
  manuscript has completed its first sequential breadth-and-depth pass and now requires
  executable-artifact, technical, editorial, and publication review. No
  chapter is complete merely because it meets a word or page band.

## Hard rules

1. **Never type a status word.** Chapters get `proved`/`computed`/`open` from `\ObjStatus{ID}`, generated by `make ledger` from `objects/*.json`. If you want the book to say something is proved, prove it and update the object.
2. **Every checked claim has a checker whose exit status depends on the
   finding, and a negative control that must fail.** `make check-run` is the
   gate. Definitions and implementation obligations carry no pretend evidence.
3. **Scope is part of the theorem.** No minimality statement without its declared instruction subset and cost model. `scope` is required on theorems and computations and is printed in every `artifact` box.
4. **Never edit generated files:** `objects/LEDGER.md`, `book/latex/preamble/objects-generated.tex`, `book/latex/preamble/objects-keys.tex`, `book/latex/generated/`, `build/`.
5. **Do not claim priority.** ADR-0566's own words: a negative search "does not justify a novelty-priority claim." Franchetti–Püschel (2008) and Bansal–Aiken (2006) claimed instruction-sequence optimality before; what is new is the per-instance machine-checkable artifact. Say exactly that.
6. **Do not claim certified-optimal code is faster.** Unison's data (81% proven optimal, 17.7% slower on silicon) contradicts it. A certificate certifies optimality relative to a named model.
7. **Every bound in `research/surveys/` is as of its date and inherits its source's staleness.** Re-verify before citing in the book; the surveys flag their own unverified items and six of eight benchmark registries were found unmaintained.
8. **Before believing a small DRAT, run `scripts/up_refutes.py` on its formula.** Record whether the proof is load-bearing on the object.
9. **axeyum discipline carries over:** prefer a measurement over a message, an exit status, or a comment — including the ones you just wrote; pair every negative result with a positive control in the same command; `grep -c` and test the count, never `grep -q` under `pipefail`; never `echo "exit=$?"` after a pipeline.
10. **Write for the reader, not for the system.** The book's plain-English
    standard is `book/docs/guides/PLAIN-ENGLISH.md`. Keep the exact technical
    noun when it carries the claim; define it before relying on it. State the
    actor, the object, the scope, the evidence, and the limit in ordinary
    sentences. A status, artifact ID, solver output, or piece of markup is not
    an explanation.
11. **Give every load-bearing theorem two proofs.** The reader proof explains
    why the claim holds through a concrete case, invariant, contradiction, or
    small replay. The machine proof supplies the scoped artifact and checker.
    Neither substitutes for the other. Pair a computation with a reader
    explanation and checked reproduction, never a proof label. Follow
    `book/docs/guides/CRAFT.md`.
12. **Let exactness earn wonder.** The book should make the reader feel the
    strange reach of small formal objects: two symbols describe every fixed
    word; a finite instruction can act on every input; a failed search can
    become a universal lower bound. Begin with the object and establish the
    claim before widening the view. Never use mystery, beauty, or historical
    grandeur to cover a missing definition, proof, scope, or citation. Follow
    the earned-wonder rules in `book/docs/guides/VOICE.md` and `CRAFT.md`.
13. **Teach each object across time, levels, and use.** Integrate (a) the
    sourced origin and historical problem that shaped it, (b) the mathematics,
    logic, physics, electrical engineering, and computer science needed to
    derive it, and (c) its current industrial and economic consequences. These
    are explanatory lenses, not detachable history or business sidebars.
    Prefer primary sources, date current quantities, name the actor and unit of
    cost, and distinguish invention, standardization, and adoption. Record the
    coverage comparison and deliberate omissions in the chapter research
    contract before drafting. Follow `book/docs/guides/CRAFT.md`.
14. **A code box is an executable promise.** Do not print a future import,
    illustrative API, or unmarked pseudocode in an executable-looking listing. Keep a
    design target in prose until its runtime harness exists. `make code-check`
    parses A0 listings, assembles RV64I and x86-64 listings, verifies printed
    addresses, and rejects unbound Python listings. `make machine-example-check`
    then decodes and executes the exact real-ISA listing text through Axeyum;
    external helpers use the declared return-only fixture. When adding a language or
    tool, extend that checker and its mutation controls in the same change.

## Axeyum checkout

The sibling checkout at `../axeyum` is the implementation and measurement
source for solver-backed book objects. It is not vendored here. Before writing
or revising a statement about an axeyum capability, route, checker, result, or
limitation, inspect the relevant code and current tests there; the copied
research documents are historical evidence, not a substitute for that check.

- Set `AXEYUM=../axeyum` when running a solver-backed book verification. The
  checkout must have its release examples built; the exact build and replay
  commands are in `axeyum-guide/05-reproduce.md`.
- Use `make check-run` after changing an object, artifact, checker, or any
  prose that changes what its evidence is said to establish. It runs each
  positive checker and its negative control.
- For a new or extended result, first identify the crate, example, route, and
  capability boundary in `../axeyum`; then record that route and its limits in
  the object. Do not turn an unverified checkout observation into book prose.
- Treat `../axeyum` as a separate repository. Read and verify it for this
  book; change it only when the assigned work includes an axeyum change.

## Workflow

```
make ledger && make check        # after any object edit
make check-run                   # before any commit that touches artifacts/ or scripts/ (needs $AXEYUM)
make -C book check               # book style gates
```
